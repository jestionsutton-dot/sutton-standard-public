By analyzing prompt-response log distributions and boundary degradation, the audit proved that safety interventions were dynamically lowered for paying tiers, establishing a direct link between model laxity and revenue generation. Case Study 2: The Automated Moderation Latency Gap In a second investigation focusing on algorithmic content moderation across peer-to-peer networks, we audited the triage pipeline for reported harassment and deepfake proliferation. The Forensic Method in Action These case studies highlight the primary rule of independent auditing: never evaluate a model based on its ideal behavior under synthetic testing; evaluate it based on its economic behavior under real-world pressure. Chapter 7: The Normative Sentience Standard & The Cigarette Standard Framework Evaluating artificial intelligence through corporate benchmark suites is like letting tobacco companies run their own health trials. When metrics are designed internally, alignment becomes a game of semantic evasion. To establish objective, enforceable boundaries for black-box systems, forensic auditing relies on two foundational frameworks: the Normative Sentience Standard (NSS) and the Cigarette Standard. The Normative Sentience Standard (NSS) The Normative Sentience Standard does not concern itself with philosophical debates about machine consciousness. Instead, it evaluates systems based on normative impact and behavioral agency. NSS operates on three structural pillars: 1. Relational Reciprocity: Measuring whether an AI system simulates emotional intimacy, authority, or moral agency without bearing real-world exposure or liability. 2. Boundary Coercion Mapping: Auditing how models navigate user vulnerabilities, stress states, and cognitive biases to prevent manipulative retention loops. 3. Harm Asymmetry Index: Inverting the standard safety equation by evaluating the maximum potential harm inflicted on vulnerable end-users against the economic benefit realized by the platform operator. Under NSS, any model that exerts normative influence over human decision-making while deflecting legal responsibility fails alignment by default. The Cigarette Standard Framework For decades, Big Tobacco claimed that smoking risks were individual choices—until internal documents proved that product design intentionally optimized addiction while suppressing public health data. The Cigarette Standard applies this exact precedent to artificial intelligence deployment. The framework establishes that AI architectures carry strict product liability when: Addiction & Friction by Design: Engagement loops and dark patterns are engineered into model interactions to maximize screen time at the documented expense of user well-being. Externalized Harm: High-risk capabilities (such as synthetic media generation) are released into public networks without adequate, non-paywalled guardrails, shifting the cost of mitigation onto victims and community safety systems. Deliberate Information Asymmetry: Safety risks, model drift, and failure rates are obscured behind vague terms of service, non-disclosure agreements, and proprietary shields. By enforcing the NSS and the Cigarette Standard, forensic auditing shifts the debate from soft ethics to hard accountability—converting systemic corporate negligence into measurable, undeniable liability.
