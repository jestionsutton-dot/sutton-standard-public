2. Primary Vulnerability: Unconscionable Liquidated Damages (§ 10.2)

The Mechanism: PayPal routinely assesses flat $2,500.00 USD administrative penalties directly against merchant and user balances for alleged AUP violations, alongside mandatory 180-day reserve holds.

The Legal Breach: Under U.C.C. § 2-718(1) and Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 356(1), liquidated damages must be reasonable in light of the anticipated or actual harm caused by the breach, the difficulties of proof of loss, and the inconvenience or nonfeasibility of otherwise obtaining an adequate remedy. A term fixing unreasonably large liquidated damages is void as a penalty.

Applying a rigid $2,500 deduction across arbitrary infractions—independent of transaction volume or actual provable damages—constitutes an unlawful private forfeiture scheme.

3. Secondary Vulnerability: Algorithmic Black-Box Freezes & UDAAP

The Mechanism: User capital is seized instantaneously by opaque machine learning risk models with zero human-readable justification or procedural avenue for timely appeal.

The Regulatory Breach: This practice violates CFPB Dodd-Frank Act § 1036 (12 U.S.C. § 5536) prohibiting Unfair, Deceptive, or Abusive Acts or Practices (UDAAP), as well as the Electronic Fund Transfer Act (EFTA 15 U.S.C. § 1693) error-resolution frameworks.
